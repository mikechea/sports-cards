Why offers sit below comps

Fees: eBay sports cards take 13.25% + $0.40 per order over $10 (≈ $13.65 on a $100 sale). Holding time and risk: the card may sit, and the price can drop while it does. Overhead and profit. Liquid cards get a higher share. Slow movers and bulk get much less.

Data points, not rules: one shop owner pays ~20% under comps when buying to resell. A collector reported some dealers at 50–60% of comps. CardVault's own rates aren't public. Follow the store's guidelines.

Credit > cash because "cash leaves the shop; credit comes back" (it gets spent on marked-up stock). Example: $10 card = $5 cash or $6.50 credit.

Red flags → pause and escalate

Counterfeit / reprintCard stock feels thin, flimsy, or stiff; blurry print or inkjet lines; chrome feels textured; silver tone off on a Silver Prizm. Compare with a known real copy.
AlteredLifted sticker-auto edges, swapped patch, re-glued relic window
TrimmedUneven edges, odd corners, wrong size vs. a known copy. PSA won't number-grade trimmed or recolored cards.
Fake slabCert lookup (psacard.com/cert), and the card must match the record (fakes reuse real certs). Check for frosted edges, flex, glue or pry marks, bright-light label test.
DealSeller eager to take far below comps on a big card

Notice, compare, get a second opinion. Never accuse. ID and reporting rules: follow store policy (unverified whether CA's secondhand-dealer reporting system covers card shops).

Script

"Here's what it's actually sold for lately in this condition. We have to resell it, which means fees and time, and the price can move while it sits, so cash is $X, or $Y in store credit. The rest we'd take as a lot for $Z."
