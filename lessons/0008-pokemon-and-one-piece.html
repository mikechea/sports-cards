Lesson 8 · ~10 minutes

Pokémon and One Piece

The other two products on the shelf, read with the same four levers.

CardVault's cases are not only sports. The shop sells Pokémon sealed product, singles, and graded cards,[1] and it sells One Piece booster boxes too.[2] The Key Holder posting still wants you "knowledgeable on all products offered."[3] Lessons 1 and 2 used Pokémon as a bridge into sports. This lesson is the version you can say out loud when the customer is holding the Pokémon or the One Piece card.

The four levers, translated

There is no rookie card in either game. The job the rookie lever does in sports (a checkable "first") shows up only on old Pokémon. Everything else maps straight across.

LeverPokémonOne Piece
WhoThe Pokémon. Charizard, Pikachu, and the Eeveelutions carry demand the way a star player does.The character. Luffy moves a card. A background pirate usually does not.
FirstA 1st Edition stamp on Wizards-era cards (1999–2003). Modern cards do not have one.No equivalent. Early sets such as OP-01 can be desirable, but that is age and demand, not a stamp.
ScarcityThe rarity symbol. Illustration rares and special illustration rares are the modern chase. A parallel foil is only a finish.Alt-art, then Special Card, Treasure Rare, and Super Alt-Art. The print run is not printed on the card.
ConditionPSA, CGC, and Beckett, on the same 1–10 idea. A 10 and an 8 of the same card are different markets.Same graders. A lot of copies stay raw because people play with them.
A fifth thing sports cards barely have

Both games are played. A card can be expensive because it wins games right now, even when the art is ordinary. Say which demand you're looking at: play or the art. They get different comps.

Pokémon

One maker

The Pokémon Company International publishes the English game. There is no Panini-style unlicensed rival putting Charizard on a card. If it isn't theirs, it isn't an official Pokémon card. The current era is the Mega Evolution series, which opened with the Mega Evolution expansion on September 26, 2025.[4]

Read the rarity symbol

The Scarlet & Violet redesign is still how you read a modern card. The Pokémon Company published the symbols in 2023, and Mega Evolution still uses illustration rares and special illustration rares.[5][4]

What you'll seeOfficial nameWhat it means at the counter
Black circle, diamond, or one starCommon, uncommon, rareBulk. A foil pattern on these is a parallel foil. Collectors still say "reverse holo." Shine here is not scarcity.
Two black starsDouble rareA regular Pokémon ex. Playable, usually not the chase.
Two silver starsUltra rareFull-art foil of an ex or a Supporter. Real, and a step down from the illustration chases.
One gold starIllustration rareAlternate full-art of a common, uncommon, or rare. This is where modern collecting starts.
Two gold starsSpecial illustration rareAlternate full-art of an ex or a Supporter. The card people mean when they say "the chase" of a set.
Three gold stars, gildedHyper rareThe gold card in Scarlet & Violet-era sets. The collector number sits past the set size (a 188/132 is a secret rare).

Mega Evolution-era sets added a gold rarity above the special illustration rares. Price guides call it a Mega Hyper Rare; some pre-release writeups said Mega Ultra Rare. The Pokémon Company's own launch note didn't use either name, and it does not publish odds.[4] Name the printing you see, then comp that exact card. A number like 188/132 means "past the set size," which is how you spot a secret rare. It is not a print run. Nobody stamped "copy 7 of 25" on it.

What to hand them

Nothing on a Pokémon box promises a special illustration rare. An English booster box is the rip (shop listings put Delta Reign's at 36 packs).[9] An Elite Trainer Box is packs plus sleeves and dice, which makes it the usual gift. A battle deck is a finished 60-card deck.

They sayHand them
"He likes Pokémon and we have about $30."A tin, a blister, or a battle deck. Lots of cards or a game tonight, not a box chase.
"I want to play."A battle deck or the Deck Crafter's Collection, then the singles the list still needs.
"I want to open something."A booster bundle or an Elite Trainer Box. Say the chase is possible, not promised.
"I want that one card."The single. Same advice as Lesson 2.
"Is this a first edition Charizard?"Look for the 1st Edition stamp. Modern Charizards don't have one. Then comp that exact printing and grade.

One Piece

One maker, and the box doesn't promise the hit

Bandai makes the One Piece Card Game. English started with OP-01 Romance Dawn in December 2022. The current main set is OP-17, The World's Strongest Warriors, released August 28, 2026, at $4.99 a pack.[10] Bandai's page lists the rarities and says the cards are a random selection. It does not print "1 secret rare per box" the way a Topps hobby box prints "1 auto or relic."

English boxes are sold as 24 packs of 12 cards. That's the configuration on CardVault's OP-13 box.[11] Japanese packs are a different product (fewer cards per pack). Read the box in your hand before you quote what's inside.

2026's English main sets, so you recognize the names on the shelf: OP-14 The Azure Sea's Seven (Jan 16), OP-15 Adventure on Kami's Island (Apr 3), OP-16 The Time of Battle (Jun 12), OP-17 (Aug 28).[12] Extra boosters are smaller themed sets. CardVault carries EB-03 Heroines Edition.[2]

What to call the chase

Bandai's words and the floor's words differ. Use theirs, and understand ours.[10]

On the floorBandai's termWhat it is
LeaderLeader cardThe card a deck is built around. It has play value and, in a popular character, collector value.
Alt artAlt-ArtSame card, special art or finish. The parallel.
SPSpecial CardA special design, called out separately from alt-arts. Scarce. Don't invent a pull rate.
Manga rareSuper Alt-ArtThe whole card is covered in manga artwork. This is the grail printing. OP-17's premium Leader version is a Super Leader Alt-Art of Monkey D. Luffy.
Treasure RareTreasure RareIts own line on the official rarity list. Comp that printing by itself.
DON!!DON!! cardThe resource card you play with, closer to an Energy than to a hit. Some have fancy finishes.

Base rarities under those, from common up: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Super Rare, Secret Rare. A Secret Rare is a real pull and usually not the card that makes a set famous. The manga-art Super Alt-Art is.

What to hand them

They sayHand them
"I just started the show."A starter deck if you have one, or a few packs. A $300 box is the wrong first purchase.
"I want to build a deck."Singles for the leader they like. Play value is the point, so sell the list, not a random box.
"I want to chase the manga Luffy."You can sell the OP-17 box. Tell them a Super Alt-Art is not guaranteed in it. If they want that card, sell the single.
"Japanese or English?"Ask which they collect, then comp that language only.

Two checks before you quote a price

Language. English and Japanese are different products with different checklists and different sold prices. A Japanese chase comp cannot price an English card, and the reverse is just as wrong.

The exact printing. Base Super Rare Luffy, alt-art Luffy, and Super Alt-Art Luffy are three cards. Same character, same show, different scarcity. Lesson 3's rule still holds: sold comps of that printing, in that grade.

Real-world rep (10 minutes)

On CardVault's site, or in the store, pick one Pokémon product and one One Piece product. For each, say the maker, the format, who you'd hand it to, and whether any hit is promised on the box. Then open a sold listing of a special illustration rare and a Super Alt-Art and notice that the character and the printing are doing different jobs.
